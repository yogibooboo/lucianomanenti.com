Calcolo Enigmatico Luciano (Cross Figure)

A mathematical cryptarithm in the classic puzzle-magazine style: nine numbers in a 3×3 matrix, with every digit hidden behind a letter. The same letter always stands for the same digit, and the three horizontal operations and the three vertical ones must all balance at the same time. You solve it by elimination using the possibility matrix, just as you would with a pencil. Three difficulty levels, puzzles that always have a unique solution, hints that explain the reasoning, best-time records and automatic game saving.

Luciano Dots and Boxes

The squared-paper game played at school: players take turns joining one dot to the next, and whoever closes a box claims it and plays again. That rule is what decides games, because a whole chain of boxes gets banked in a single turn. It takes half a minute to learn, but there is real strategy behind it: the goal is to leave your opponent without a safe move, and it is sometimes worth declining the last two boxes of a chain to force them to open the next one. Three grid sizes (5×5, 8×8 and 11×11) plus a custom one up to 12×14, a computer opponent with three levels — the strongest knows chain play and uses the sacrifice — games won and best margin per level, and autosave.

Luciano Secret Code

The deduction puzzle handed down from the old Bulls and Cows: the computer hides a colour code and you have to work it out, one guess at a time. Every row is answered with two figures only — how many colours are in the right slot and how many are the right colour in the wrong slot — and from there it is all elimination, never guesswork. Three levels (4 slots and 6 colours, 4 slots and 8 colours, 5 slots and 8 colours), with the option of banning repeated colours; a possibility matrix to record what you have already ruled out, three optional helpers you can switch on as you like, hints that explain the reasoning, best-guess records and autosave.
