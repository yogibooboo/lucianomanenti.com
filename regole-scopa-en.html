Scopa Rules

See here the rules of the Brescian Variant.

Scopa is one of the most famous, traditional, and beloved card games in Italy. It is played with a deck of 40 cards (traditionally Neapolitan, or a reduced French deck, with values from Ace to King, where the Ace is worth 1, cards 2 to 7 keep their face value, and the Jack, Queen, and King are worth 8, 9, and 10 respectively).

It can be played by 2 players (heads-up) or 4 players (two fixed partnerships). In this online version, you can challenge the AI in both 1v1 and 2v2 (team-based) modes.

This game is continuously evolving: new features and improvements are added regularly. In this online version, the avatars of your opponents and your partner are chosen randomly at the start of the game and remain persistent throughout your browser session. If you notice anything not working as expected or have suggestions, please write to us at postmaster@lucianomanenti.com — your feedback is highly appreciated!

1. Objective of the Game

The goal is to reach or exceed the target score established at the beginning of the match (usually 11 or 21 points) before the opponents. To do this, players or teams accumulate points at the end of each round (smazzata) through captures, Scope, and card counts.

2. Setup and Deal

  • A starting dealer is chosen randomly and rotates counter-clockwise after each round.
  • The dealer shuffles the deck and deals 3 face-down cards to each player.
  • 4 face-up cards are placed in the center of the table.
  • The rest of the deck is placed face down to the side for subsequent deals (3 cards to each player every time they run out of cards in hand, until the deck is exhausted).

3. Svolgimento del Gioco (Gameplay)

The game flows in a counter-clockwise direction starting from the player to the dealer's right. On their turn, each player is required to play one card from their hand onto the table, either to make a capture or to discard it.

The Capture (La Presa)

There are two ways to capture cards from the table:

  • Direct Capture (Semplice): If the played card matches the rank of a card on the table (e.g., playing a 5 when there is a 5 on the table), the player captures both.
  • Sum Capture: If the played card matches the sum of two or more cards on the table, the player captures them all (e.g., playing a 7 to capture a 4 and a 3, or a 5 and a 2).

Official Single Card Capture Rule: If there is a single card matching the rank of the played card (e.g., a 6) AND a combination of cards summing to that value (e.g., a 4 and a 2), the player is obligated to capture the single card (the 6). A sum capture is only allowed if no single card of the same rank is present on the table.

If the played card does not match any card or sum on the table, the player makes no capture and the card remains face up on the table, joining the other discarded cards.

4. The Scopa

If a player manages to capture all cards present on the table with a single play, leaving it completely empty, they score a Scopa.

  • A Scopa is worth 1 point immediately.
  • The card that scored the Scopa is placed sideways or face up in the player's taking pile as a reminder of the point.
  • Exception: It is not possible to score a Scopa on the last play of the last hand of the round (the final play gathers the remaining cards on the table for the player who made the last capture, but does not score a Scopa).

5. End of the Round

When the cards in hand run out and the deck is exhausted, the round ends. The last player who made a valid capture on the table gathers any remaining cards on the table and adds them to their team's taking pile for the final count (this final capture does not score a Scopa).

6. Scoring

At the end of each round, points are calculated for each team or player:

Point Description Points
Scope 1 point for each Scopa scored during the round. +1 each
Cards (Carte) Awarded to whoever captured the majority of the cards (at least 21 cards out of 40). In case of a tie (20-20), the point is not awarded. +1
Diamonds (Quadri) Awarded to whoever captured the majority of Diamonds/Denari (at least 6 cards out of 10). In case of a tie (5-5), the point is not awarded. +1
Settebello Awarded to whoever captured the 7 of Diamonds (7♦). +1
Primiera Awarded to whoever achieves the highest Primiera score, calculated by summing the Primiera values of the best card in each of the 4 suits. In case of a tie, the point is not awarded. +1

How to Play "Scopa by Luciano"

  • Select a card: click on one of the three cards in your hand to select it.
  • Automatic Discard and Capture:
    • If no captures are possible with the selected card, it is automatically discarded onto the table after a short delay.
    • If there is only one possible capture on the table (e.g., playing a 5 with only a 5 on the board), the capture is executed automatically after a short delay.
  • Multiple Captures: If the selected card can make multiple different captures (e.g., playing a 7 when there is a 7, a 5, and a 2 on the table), the game waits for you to choose your preferred combination by clicking on the cards on the board. Once you have selected the desired cards on the table, click the golden PRENDI (Capture) button that appears in the center of the screen.
  • Glowing Yellow Border: The active player's area is surrounded by a flashing neon yellow border, helping you instantly identify whose turn it is.
  • Real-Time Threshold Alerts: In the score panel on the left, the current hand's Card and Diamond counters turn bold yellow as soon as a team passes the mathematical majority threshold (21 for cards, 6 for diamonds), accompanied by a chime sound.

Control Buttons

  • NUOVA (New): Interrupts the current game, resets the scores, and reopens the starting panel to choose the game mode (2P or 4P) and target score.
  • SCOPRI CARTE / COPRI CARTE (Reveal/Hide Cards): Shows or hides the cards of the opponents and partner managed by the computer (ideal for training or debugging purposes).
  • 🔊 / 🔇 (Audio): Enables or disables the game's sound effects. Your preference is global and will be remembered by the browser across all pages on the site.

Brescian Scopa Rules

Brescian Scopa is a variant of Scopa that is both intriguing and rich in scoring opportunities. It is played with the Brescian deck of 52 cards (4 suits: Swords, Coins, Cups, Clubs — with values from Ace to King, where the face cards Jack, Knight, and King have no numeric value for sums). The scoring system is quite different from the classic version, with many additional points tied to Swords and special cards.

1. Differences from Classic Scopa

  • Played with 52 cards (full Brescian deck, face cards included).
  • 4 cards are dealt to each player instead of 3.
  • Face cards (Jack, Knight, King) can only capture the face card of the same rank — they cannot be used in sums.
  • For numeric cards, a single-card capture is not mandatory: if both a matching single card and a sum combination are available on the table, the player may freely choose which to take.
  • The Primiera is not calculated.
  • The match score is obtained by adding all points scored in each round.

2. End-of-Round Points

PointDescriptionPoints
Scope / Piccate / Simili / Quadriglie All these special points (described below) are summed together. +1 each
Cards (27+) Awarded to whoever collected at least 27 cards out of 52. In case of a tie, the point is not awarded. +1
Swords (7+) Awarded to whoever collected at least 7 Sword cards out of 13. +1
Napola Scored by collecting the Ace, 2 and 3 of Swords. Worth 3 base points, plus 1 additional point for each subsequent card in sequence (4, 5, 6... of Swords). 3+
2 of Swords Special card: worth 1 point to whoever captures it. +1
10 of Coins Special card: worth 1 point to whoever captures it. +1
Jack of Cups Special card: worth 1 point to whoever captures it. +1

3. Special Points During Play

Scopa

As in the classic version: a Scopa is scored when all cards on the table are captured in a single play, leaving it empty. Worth 1 point. A Scopa cannot be scored on the last play of the round.

Picca

Scored when a player captures only the card just played by the previous opponent, without combining it with other cards already on the table. Worth 1 point. If a Scopa is also scored at the same time, it becomes a Picca e Scopa (+2 points). Picca does not apply across a redeal between hands.

Simile

Scored when all cards in a capture (the played card plus those taken from the table) belong to the same suit. The capture must involve at least 2 cards from the table. Worth 1 point. Cumulative with Quadriglia.

Quadriglia / Cinquina

Scored by capturing at least 3 cards from the table in a single play. Worth 1 point regardless of the exact number (3 or more). With 4 or more cards it is called Cinquina but the score is the same. Cumulative with Simile.

Napola (progressive)

During play, a notification appears each time a player reaches or extends their Napola sequence. The game panel shows the Sword sequence colored: green for cards captured by you, red for cards captured by the opponent. In the end-of-round table, when the Napola has become impossible (Ace, 2 and 3 of Swords split between both players), whoever did not achieve it sees the symbol X instead of zero.
