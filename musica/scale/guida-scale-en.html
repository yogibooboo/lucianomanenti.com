♪ Musical Scales Guide

This guide introduces you to musical scale recognition by ear. You will learn what a scale is, the structural differences between the various scales in the exercise, how the interface works, and practical strategies to train your ear.

1. What is a scale?

A musical scale is an ordered sequence of notes (called degrees) within an octave. The interval sequence (expressed in tones and semitones) determines the character or "colour" of the scale, influencing the style and emotion of the melody built upon it.

Every scale starts on a fundamental note (the root note) and develops upwards (ascending scale) or downwards (descending scale). For example, the Major scale has a fixed sequence of intervals: 2 - 2 - 1 - 2 - 2 - 2 - 1 semitones (Whole - Whole - Half - Whole - Whole - Whole - Half).

📌 Root Note This is the scale's gravitational center, the note that provides resolution. In the exercise, the root note is highlighted in yellow on the piano keyboard and the staff at the start of each turn.

3. How the exercise works

The system proposes a randomly generated scale starting from a root note. You can guess the scale via the grid or play the notes on the virtual/physical keyboard.

Interface Elements

▶ Play Button
Plays the current scale. If you have already answered correctly, pressing it generates a new question.
⇅ Direction (Ascending / Descending / Both)
Choose how the scale is played: only upwards, only downwards, or both directions in a row.
Note: For the melodic minor scale (which ascends differently than it descends), the descending mode is disabled to prevent ambiguity.
🎹 Piano Keyboard (Active Recognition)
The root note is highlighted in **yellow**. You can answer by playing the consecutive notes of the scale: - Correct keys turn **green**. - Incorrect keys turn **red**. If you press all correct notes of the scale without making any mistakes, the exercise ends successfully and the point is awarded.
■ Scale Grid
Shows the active scales for the chosen difficulty level. Click the corresponding button to guess. If you guess right on the first try, you get the point. Afterwards, you can click on other buttons to play and compare other scales starting on the same root note.

4. MIDI Input (Physical Keyboard)

You can connect a real **USB MIDI keyboard** to your computer to perform the exercises naturally.

  • The MIDI input is always active (no configuration needed). It supports hot-plugging: you can plug in the USB cable before or after opening the browser.
  • Exact octave matching: you must play the notes in the exact octave shown on the screen. If the displayed root is C4 (60), you must start playing from the middle C of your physical keyboard. Notes played in different octaves will be ignored.
  • All notes pressed on the physical keyboard are immediately reflected on the virtual keyboard on screen, turning green (if correct) or red (if wrong).

5. Ear Training Tips

Listening to key degrees

Many modes and scales differ by just one note compared to the major or natural minor scale. Pay attention to these telltale degrees:

  • If you hear a major scale but it sounds slightly "strange" or "spacey", listen to the 4th degree: if raised, it's a **Lydian**. If it has a blues/rock vibe on the end instead, check the 7th degree: if lowered, it's a **Mixolydian**.
  • If you listen to a minor scale, check the 6th degree: if it sounds higher (more open) than usual, it's a **Dorian**. If the beginning immediately feels dark (lowered 2nd degree), it's a **Phrygian**.

Melodic Minor Scale

Remember that the melodic minor scale is asymmetrical: it ascends with a raised 6th and 7th degree (closer to a major scale with a minor third) and descends like a natural minor. In the exercise, if you select ascending or both directions, you must play the ascending version on the keyboard to complete the exercise.
