15 Puzzle Rules

The 15 Puzzle is the classic sliding puzzle made of a four-by-four square frame containing fifteen tiles numbered from 1 to 15 and one empty space.

Goal of the game

The goal is to bring all fifteen tiles back into numerical order from left to right and top to bottom: 1, 2, 3, 4 in the first row, 5, 6, 7, 8 in the second, 9, 10, 11, 12 in the third and 13, 14, 15 in the fourth, leaving the empty square in the bottom-right corner.

How to play on this page

You can slide any tile that sits in the same row or the same column as the empty space:

  • With the mouse or touch — click a tile next to the empty square to slide it in. If you click a tile further away in the same row or column as the gap, all the tiles in between slide together in a single move.
  • With the keyboard — use the four arrow keys to slide an adjacent tile into the empty space in the direction of the arrow you press.

The tiles come in two alternating colours (red and ivory) so that when the board is in order from 1 to 15 they form a neat checkerboard pattern. In addition, tiles that are already in their final home square have a gold border, so you can see at a glance which squares are settled.

The three shuffle levels

Not every random arrangement of fifteen tiles can be solved: half of all permutations are mathematically impossible. On this page every board is guaranteed to be solvable: the first two levels start from the solved board and make a sequence of valid random moves, the third one discards the impossible arrangements.

  • Easy — the board is shuffled with 15 valid moves from the solved position: great for warming up or playing a quick round.
  • Normal — 35 shuffle moves: enough to scatter the tiles across the frame without wandering too far.
  • Full — the tiles are placed completely at random, as in the classic 15 Puzzle challenge.

The timer starts with your first move, not when the board appears: take all the time you need to study it.

How to solve it every time: the row-by-row method

The trick to never undoing what you have already built is to work from the top down, one row at a time:

  1. First row (1, 2, 3, 4) — put 1 and 2 in place first, which is straightforward. For 3 and 4, do not push 3 into its corner on its own: instead park 4 in square 3 and bring 3 right below it, then slide both of them into the top-right corner as a train. From then on, never touch the first row again.
  2. Second row (5, 6, 7, 8) — repeat the exact same technique in the remaining 3×4 rectangle: place 5 and 6, then pair up 7 and 8 to rotate them together into the right end of the second row.
  3. Last two rows (9–15) — only eight squares remain. Settle the leftmost column first by pairing 9 and 13 (park 13 in square 9's corner and bring 9 next to it so both rotate into place together), then do the same for 10 and 14. The last three tiles (11, 12, 15) simply cycle around the final 2×2 corner and fall into place in two or three moves.
